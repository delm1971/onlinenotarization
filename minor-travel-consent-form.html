Minor Travel Consent Form: What It Is and How to Get One

A parent-friendly guide to child travel permission letters, international travel considerations, notarization, and a pre-trip checklist.

A minor travel consent form—also called a child travel consent letter—is written permission from a parent or legal guardian for a child to travel with one parent, another adult, or sometimes alone. It can help demonstrate that the trip is authorized, particularly for international travel.

Travel reminder: Requirements vary by destination, airline, border authority, and custody situation. The U.S. government recommends checking the destination country's rules and says a consent letter is preferred in English and notarized for certain travel situations.

What is a minor travel consent form?

The form identifies the child, the adult accompanying the child, the travel dates and destination, and the parent or guardian giving permission. It may also include contact information and an emergency contact. A consent letter does not replace a child's passport, visa, custody order, or other required travel documents.

When might a minor travel consent form be needed?

  • A child travels internationally with only one parent
  • A child travels with a grandparent, relative, coach, or other adult who is not a parent
  • A child travels alone as an unaccompanied minor, subject to airline rules
  • A destination country, airline, cruise operator, or other authority requests written parental permission

The United States does not generally require evidence of both parents' permission for a minor to leave the country, but some destination countries may require it. Check official guidance for the countries involved.

What information should the consent letter include?

  1. Child's full legal name and date of birth
  2. Passport details for international travel, if appropriate
  3. Name and contact information of the parent or guardian granting permission
  4. Name, relationship, and contact details of the accompanying adult
  5. Destination, departure and return dates, and travel itinerary if available
  6. A clear statement that the parent gives permission for the trip
  7. Emergency contact details
  8. Signature and date lines, plus a notary section if notarization is required or recommended

Should a minor travel consent form be notarized?

For international travel, USAGov says a consent letter is preferred in English and notarized in situations where a child travels without custodial parents or with a guardian. The U.S. Department of State also advises travelers to check destination-specific requirements; some countries require a notarized letter from the non-traveling parent or proof of sole custody.

Notarization can help verify the parent's signature, but it does not guarantee that a border official or airline will accept the letter. Contact the destination's embassy or consulate and the airline before departure.

How to get a minor travel consent form notarized online

  1. Prepare the consent letter using the trip details and any official template requested.
  2. Confirm whether the destination or airline requires notarization, witnesses, translation, or additional authentication.
  3. Check whether remote online notarization is accepted for the document and location.
  4. Have the parent or guardian sign according to the notary's instructions and provide accepted identification.
  5. Keep the completed consent letter with the child's travel documents and carry any relevant custody or relationship documents.

Sample consent wording

A simple permission statement may say: “I acknowledge that my child, [child's full name], is traveling to [destination] with [adult's full name] from [date] to [date] with my permission.”

This is only an example of wording, not a universal legal form. Add the details required by your destination, airline, and custody situation, and use any official form they provide.

Checklist before the trip

  • Child's valid passport or other required travel document
  • Consent letter with accurate trip and adult information
  • Notarization if requested or recommended
  • Birth certificate or proof of parent-child relationship, if useful or required
  • Custody order or proof of sole custody when applicable
  • Airline unaccompanied-minor arrangements and fees, if relevant
  • Destination country's entry and exit rules confirmed

Frequently asked questions

Does a minor travel consent form need to be notarized?

It depends on the destination and travel situation. U.S. government guidance says notarization is preferred for certain consent letters, and some countries require it.

Can one parent travel internationally with a child?

It may be possible, but some destinations require consent from the non-traveling parent or proof of sole custody. Check the destination's rules before departure.

Can I notarize a minor travel consent form online?

Possibly, if remote notarization is permitted for the document and accepted by the destination or organization requesting it. Confirm before using an online notary.

Does a consent letter replace a child's passport?

No. A consent letter is separate from a passport, visa, custody documents, and other required travel paperwork
