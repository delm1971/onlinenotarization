Power of Attorney Notarization: Requirements and Process

Find out what notarization does, how the process works, and what to confirm before signing your power of attorney.

Power of attorney notarization is the process of having a notary verify the signing or acknowledgment of a POA. The exact steps depend on the document, your state's execution rules, and the organization that will rely on the POA.

Key point: The notary verifies the notarial act—not whether your POA gives the right powers or is legally sufficient for your situation. Confirm the form and acceptance requirements before signing.

What is power of attorney notarization?

A power of attorney names an agent to act for a principal. Notarization can help authenticate the principal's signature and complete the required notarial certificate. Some POAs also require witnesses, and some institutions have their own forms or acceptance procedures.

Step-by-step: notarize a power of attorney

Step 1: Confirm the form and requirements

Ask the receiving institution whether it has a preferred POA form, whether notarization is required, and whether witnesses must be present. For property or financial matters, confirm any recording or delivery requirements.

Step 2: Prepare the document

Fill in the requested information but follow the signing instructions. Do not make unauthorized changes to a form or leave required sections incomplete.

Step 3: Gather ID and witnesses

Have an accepted government-issued photo ID available. If witnesses are required, make sure they meet the document's criteria and know whether they must attend the same session.

Step 4: Choose your notary method

For in-person notarization, attend the appointment and follow the notary's instructions. For remote online notarization, use a provider and notary authorized for the transaction, and check that the receiving party accepts remote notarization.

Step 5: Complete the signing and notarial certificate

Follow the notary's instructions for signing or acknowledging the signature. Review the certificate for missing dates, names, seals, or other required information before leaving or ending the session.

Step 6: Deliver and retain copies

Provide the signed POA to the organization that needs it. Keep a secure copy and share it with your agent as appropriate. Ask whether the institution needs an original, certified copy, or electronic record.

Online power of attorney notarization

Online notarization can be convenient when permitted, especially if the principal and agent are in different locations or scheduling an in-person appointment is difficult. A typical remote process may include uploading the document, verifying identity, connecting by live audio-video, signing or acknowledging the document, and receiving the completed record.

Do not assume every POA is accepted online. State law, the notary's commission, document-specific requirements, and the receiving institution's policy may affect eligibility.

What does POA notarization cost?

Costs depend on the notary method and number of required notarial acts. A local notary may charge fees governed by state rules; a mobile notary may add a travel fee. An online provider may charge a platform transaction price plus fees for additional seals, witnesses, or signers. Check the current total before proceeding.

POA notarization checklist

  • Correct POA form for your purpose and jurisdiction
  • Receiving institution's requirements confirmed
  • All required fields completed
  • Valid ID ready
  • Witnesses arranged if required
  • Signing method accepted by the receiving party
  • Completed document reviewed and safely stored

When should you seek legal help?

Consider an attorney if you are unsure what powers to grant, whether the POA should be durable or limited, when it should take effect, or how it interacts with an existing estate plan. A notary cannot provide those legal decisions for you.

Frequently asked questions

What is power of attorney notarization?

It is a notarial act connected to signing or acknowledging a POA. It does not replace the legal requirements for creating the document.

How long does it take to notarize a POA online?

Timing varies with document readiness, identity verification, witness needs, and platform availability. Do not rely on a guaranteed completion time.

Can a POA be notarized remotely?

Remote notarization may be available when authorized by law and accepted by the organization receiving the POA.

What if the POA requires witnesses?

Arrange witnesses who meet the document's requirements and confirm whether they must be present in person or can participate remotely
